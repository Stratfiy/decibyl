## Problem Statement

Decibyl is a working agent platform for Indian businesses: voice bots on Indian
phone numbers in Hinglish, Tamil and Telugu, campaigns, QA scoring on every
call, DND scrubbing, prepaid INR billing with GST invoices, and a channels
layer (WhatsApp, web chat, email, scheduled bot runs) under construction. The
founder wants to scale it as a SaaS.

The diagnostic established that the product is **pre-product commercially**:
the software runs, payments are verified end to end, but no outside business
depends on it and nobody has ever been asked to pay with a price attached.
The scaling question is therefore not "how do we grow" but "who pays first,
for what, and how do we find them repeatably".

## Status Quo

The founder's answer was "all" of: a human doing it badly (receptionist,
telecaller, the owner's own phone), a cloud-telephony IVR that answers but does
not convert, nothing at all so the lead dies, or another AI vendor. Pushed for
one specific business, no single named user emerged.

External numbers for the human baseline in India, September 2026: a fully
loaded telecaller costs roughly ₹30,000 to ₹45,000 a month at a BPO and
₹45,000 to ₹70,000 in-house; a clinic receptionist post in a tier-1 city
typically advertises ₹12,000 to ₹20,000. Managed voice platforms land at ₹4
to ₹9 per minute, or ₹5 to ₹10 per completed call. Vendors uniformly pitch
"2.5 to 5x cheaper per connect". Sources in the Landscape section.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user for test 1:** the owner or practice manager of a clinic or
diagnostic centre in Hyderabad who has posted a receptionist or front-desk job
in the last 14 days. Direct employers only; posts from staffing agencies and
BPOs are discarded, because an agency loses the placement fee if the role is
automated.

**Why this role first, and not the founder's own domain.** Three roles were
considered: collections telecaller (outbound), procurement follow-up
(outbound, the founder's domain), and clinic receptionist (inbound). Only the
inbound role can go live tomorrow: the demo inbound path is verified on a real
call, while outbound needs a consented list, a compliant number series and
carrier approval that is still gated. Procurement is test 2, not dropped; see
Open Questions for its real job-board titles.

**Wedge:** the job in the post, done by a bot, sold as a fixed monthly top-up
anchored to the advertised salary, spent on the existing prepaid ledger so the
account never leaves the billing that already exists. No platform code changes. Reuses the
clinic templates, missed-call callback, inbound routing, QA scoring and
prepaid billing that already exist.

## Constraints

- Five people, commission-only reps, about ₹5L runway (ROADMAP.md, 2 Sept).
  At a ₹5,000 to ₹6,000 monthly ticket a commission rep earns under ₹1,000
  per close, so **the first three accounts are founder-sold**. Reps join once
  the template gallery makes setup a ten-minute job and the ticket or volume
  justifies it.
- Outbound cold-calling in India is regulated; TRAI actioned 183,839 telecom
  resources in one quarter. Test 1 is inbound only. Outreach to hiring
  managers is by LinkedIn message, email to the company domain, or the
  recruiter phone number printed on the Naukri post, and never by an
  automated dialler.
- Another agent is actively building the platform (PR #231 and successors).
  Test 1 must not depend on unshipped platform work.
- Managed telephony is gated off pending carrier approval. For the pilots,
  Decibyl pre-buys three to five Plivo numbers before Monday (Indian virtual
  numbers need KYC paperwork and are not instant) and assigns one per
  customer through the superadmin platform-managed telephony configuration,
  which already exists. One demo number cannot serve two customers' inbound,
  so this step is per account.
- Missed-call callback is the one outbound action in test 1. It rings back a
  person who just rang the clinic, from the clinic's own Decibyl number, so
  it is a service call to an inbound caller and not a promotional call. It
  stays in the offer on that basis; if the carrier or TRAI number-series
  check says otherwise before Monday, it comes out of the message and the
  bot answers only.
- Calls are recorded and QA-scored, so the greeting carries a one-line
  recording notice in Telugu and English (DPDP hygiene). Check the clinic
  template says it before Monday.

## Premises (all five agreed)

1. Decibyl is pre-product. Software exists and works; no business depends on
   it; nobody has been asked for money.
2. The status quo being replaced is a human doing repeated phone and WhatsApp
   work badly, not another AI vendor.
3. The edge is not the voice runtime (commoditised). It is INR prepaid GST
   billing, India-region latency, Indian languages, and the founder's own
   procurement and manufacturing knowledge.
4. "Co-worker plus organisation memory" is the right three-year vision and the
   wrong first sale. The first sale is one job, priced, to one named business.
5. Clinics, lenders and D2C on the marketing site were chosen from research,
   not from anyone the founder knows. Procurement teams are where the founder
   can get a yes fastest once outbound is unblocked; until then the inbound
   clinic role is the only one deliverable this week.

## Landscape (generic category searches, 14 Sept 2026)

- Layer 1, what everyone knows: AI calling is cheaper than a telecaller. Every
  Indian vendor page says so with the same 2.5x to 5x figure.
- Layer 2, current discourse: the most repeated buyer mistake is evaluating on
  the demo rather than on deployment; the most repeated founder mistake is
  building for months before talking to customers; the biggest technical
  barrier is Hinglish and regional code-switching on phone audio. "Hire an AI
  employee" is already a US category with nine stock roles (SDR, support
  agent, bookkeeper and so on), priced at a flat monthly fee.
- Layer 3, where the conventional approach is wrong here: vendors sell minutes
  to a buyer who has never computed cost per connect and never will. The
  buyer has computed exactly one number, the salary in the job post they
  wrote. **EUREKA: the job post is the demand artifact.** It names the work,
  the language, the city, the salary and the hiring manager, and the buyer
  authored it. Selling "the job you posted, done by a bot at a third of the
  salary" skips the demo-evaluation trap because the buyer already defined
  the job, and it turns job boards into a lead list that refreshes daily.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Job-post wedge, no platform code
Collect live receptionist posts by hand, message the hiring manager with a
price anchored to the salary they printed, get them live on the existing
clinic template. Effort S (human about a week, with Claude Code about a day
for message drafts and a tracking sheet). Risk low. Reuses: clinic templates,
missed-call callback, inbound routing, QA scoring, prepaid billing,
platform-managed telephony configuration.

### Approach B: Hot-jobs marketplace page
A public "jobs a bot can do" catalogue on decibyl.ai, each role showing the
advertised salary range against the bot price, backed by the packs API.
Effort M (human about three weeks, with Claude Code about two days). Risk
medium: a page is not a customer, and it builds before anyone has said yes.
Rejected as the first move; adopted as the second move once A has three
paying accounts, because by then the copy is written by real conversations.

### Approach C: Finish the co-worker platform first
Channels, organisation memory, WhatsApp, scheduled runs shipped, then sell.
Effort XL. Risk high. Rejected as the sales plan: it is the "build the full
platform before anyone can use it" pattern, and the roadmap already records
no evidence behind it. It continues as engineering work in parallel and is
not on the critical path to the first rupee.

## Recommended Approach

A now, B after A converts, C continues in the background. Founder's words:
"all: platform is in building and job posts." The decision that matters is
that A is the only thing on the critical path to revenue, and it needs no
platform change.

### The offer

One role, one city, one language pair: clinic receptionist, Hyderabad,
Telugu and English. The message quotes the post back:

> You are hiring a front-desk receptionist at ₹15,000. Decibyl answers the
> same calls in Telugu and English, books the appointment into your calendar
> and confirms it to the patient, and rings back every call you miss, for ₹5,000 a
> month including GST. It starts this week and never takes leave. Put ₹1,000
> on it to start and we match it, so you hear two thousand rupees of calls
> before the first monthly top-up.

"Books the appointment" means what the clinic template does today: a calendar
slot through the Google Calendar integration and a note to the owner. Patient
confirmation by SMS needs DLT registration (entity, header, template); until
that is verified before Monday, confirmation is spoken on the call and the
SMS line stays out of the pitch. A clinic without a Google account gets one
created on the onboarding call.

**Replacement, not overflow.** The pitch replaces the receptionist, so the
setup is unconditional forwarding of the clinic's line to its Decibyl number
during clinic hours, with the template's transfer-to-human node pointed at
the owner's mobile for anything the bot cannot handle. Overflow-only
forwarding would make the bot a ₹5,000 answering machine and break the
salary anchor.

### The price, reconciled with minutes

- Monthly top-up: one third of the advertised salary, taking the lower bound
  when the post prints a range, rounded to the nearest ₹500, floor ₹4,000,
  quoted inclusive of 18 percent GST. A ₹15,000 post gives ₹5,000.
- It is a top-up, not a flat fee. Unused credit rolls over, because the
  prepaid ledger has no expiry today and adding one is platform code. Say so
  on the call; a clinic that hears "unused minutes carry forward" hears a
  feature. Revisit expiry only if rollover balances grow past one month.
- The ₹3/min pay-as-you-go figure elsewhere in the repo is the platform fee
  alone; managed speech, model and carriage sit on top of it, which is how a
  managed Indic minute lands at ₹5.17 to ₹8.21 all-in.
- Minutes cap: the flat price divided by the account's all-in managed rate,
  currently ₹5.17 to ₹8.21 per minute depending on synthesis volume
  (COMPETITIVE-PRICING-STUDY.md, still to be replaced by the real
  characters-per-minute query). At ₹8 per minute, ₹5,000 buys about 600
  minutes, which is roughly 400 inbound clinic calls at 90 seconds each.
  A single-doctor clinic takes 150 to 400 calls a month, so ₹5,000 covers a
  clinic under roughly 300 calls once callbacks and GST are counted; above that the
  post usually prints a higher salary and the top-up scales with it.
- Mechanism: the top-up lands on the existing prepaid ledger and is spent at
  the existing rate card. No new billing mode. At zero balance the bot stops
  answering and a forwarded call would fail, so during pilots the founder
  keeps a ₹500 buffer on every account and the clinic is shown how to switch
  forwarding off from their handset in ten seconds. Auto-recharge takes over
  once Razorpay approves mandates.
- Gross margin at these numbers: the platform fee plus 1.7x managed markup
  leaves roughly 40 to 50 percent on a managed Indic minute (PRD.md §4).
  Thin, and acceptable for a demand test; not a price to scale on without
  the characters-per-minute correction.
- Trial: the clinic pays ₹1,000 on the onboarding call and Decibyl credits a
  matching ₹1,000, so money changes hands on day one and the clinic still
  hears the bot before the first monthly top-up. About 210 minutes net of
  GST, roughly 140 calls, one to two weeks for a busy clinic. The founder
  checks pilot balances in the superadmin billing overview every morning
  and messages the owner the day before it runs out.
- Ledger arithmetic: a GST-inclusive quote credits price ÷ 1.18. ₹5,000
  lands as about ₹4,240, which is about 530 minutes at ₹8, not 600; the
  clinic-size guidance above already uses the lower figure.

### The week (day 1 is Monday 21 September; Friday's three are the smoke
test and count toward the twenty)

Before Monday, a four-item check: the pre-bought numbers are live and
inbound reaches the clinic template on a real call, with the fallback that
pilot one runs on the existing verified demo number if KYC is late; Plivo's
India outbound policy allows the missed-call callback from that number; the
template greeting carries the recording notice; the Razorpay page takes
₹1,000 end to end. Note in the discovery script that call forwarding legs
are metered on the clinic's Jio or Airtel plan.
The other four people are not on this test; nobody waits on anyone.

1. Monday: collect twenty direct-employer receptionist posts in Hyderabad
   by hand from Apna, WorkIndia, Indeed, Naukri and LinkedIn into a sheet
   with company, salary, the post's language requirement, contact route and
   post date, seventeen new plus Friday's three. Count only posts with a
   reachable contact (a printed phone, an email, or a named person on
   LinkedIn); budget half a day, not an hour, and accept fifteen reachable
   posts as the floor. Google Maps clinic listings that mention hiring are
   the overflow source. No scraper; the boards forbid it.
2. Tuesday: send twenty messages, one per post, via whichever route the post
   exposes. Thursday second touch to non-repliers, two lines: "Still hiring
   for the front desk? Happy to let you hear the bot take a call in Telugu,
   two minutes, whenever suits." Nothing automated.
2b. Week two: a second batch of twenty, unconditionally, because twenty
   messages cannot produce three paying accounts by day 30 on any realistic
   funnel. The message is rewritten from whatever week one's calls taught.
3. Every reply: a fifteen-minute discovery call the same day, founder on the
   call, ending with a yes or no on the ₹1,000. A yes books a separate
   thirty-minute onboarding slot within a day.
4. Onboarding, in this order: pay the ₹1,000 through the Razorpay page and
   apply the matching credit, assign a pre-bought number, set unconditional
   forwarding from the clinic's existing line to that number for clinic
   hours, point the transfer node at the owner's mobile, place one real test
   call from the founder's phone, then watch the owner fill
   the clinic template themselves without driving. What they get stuck on
   is the product backlog. A verbal yes without the top-up does not count.

## Open Questions

- Reply rate. Unknown until sent; the second touch exists because a single
  touch is a thin test.
- Test 2 role, once outbound is unblocked: procurement follow-up in the
  founder's domain. Real job-board titles to query are "purchase executive",
  "procurement executive", "vendor coordinator" and "purchase assistant".
- Does the flat-per-job price cannibalise ₹3/min pay-as-you-go, or front it?
  Decide after three accounts, not before.
- Does the ten-minute self-serve template setup actually hold for a clinic
  owner on a phone? ROADMAP item 3 says this is the highest-ROI item; test 1
  is the first time it is watched.

## Success Criteria

- Day 7: twenty messages, two touches, at least three discovery calls held.
- Day 10: fewer than three calls held despite two or more replies means the
  message, not the source, is weak; send twenty more from the same boards
  with a rewritten message before switching source.
- Day 14: at least one clinic has paid its ₹1,000, forwarding is on, and
  real inbound calls are answered by the bot.
- Day 30: three paying accounts across the two batches, each with calls
  happening without the founder present and a named contact who would ring
  if it broke.
- Kill signal, channel: twenty messages, fewer than two replies, zero calls.
  The lead source is wrong; switch source, not product.
- Kill signal, product or price: three or more calls held, zero top-ups. The
  objection heard on the calls is the next design doc.
